# Port spec: HUD, calendar page, parameter panel, magazine, save panel (m-hud)

Roadmap phase 2, unit `m-hud` ([[tickets/T-27016-p2-m-hud]], parent [[tickets/T-27000-phase-2]]). The current objects are `src/main/hud_panels.c` and `src/main/hud_screens.c` (81 functions). `config/renames/proposals/m-hud-apply.tsv` and the exploratory table document the historical function packet and its scope; current names follow applied ledgers and source, while candidate rows without ledger entries remain proposals. The historical batch covered 45 placeholder function definitions plus seven existing-name corrections. The functions use their owning main prefixes (`portrait_`, `hud_`, `calendar_`, `message_`; event-face helpers use `portrait_`). Data, fields, types and constants have separate ownership and application histories. No emulator claim was added by the earlier reconciliation.

Scope (port first): the game rules, state and flow are described in full; drawing, VRAM and sound-bank loading are named at module level. Facts only, in this project's words; guesses are marked HYPOTHESIS. No game text is quoted: labels and titles are paraphrased.

Checks with the emulator ([[emulator]]), from a saved state on the weekday run screen of 1995-04-05 (mode 0x40, the screen that plays out the chosen command): a screenshot gave the layout in section 2; `trace` over 145 frames showed which functions run every frame (section 2); `break` on `hud_command_roll_poor_result` and `hud_biorhythm_value` gave the call chain and arguments of section 12; a write watch on `D_8011ECA4` gave section 18; memory dumps gave the start values of `place_flags`, `icon_positions`, `icon_enabled` and the mascot variables; a second state on the command screen (1995-04-09) was driven with the pad to the calendar check page (section 13). Save states and screenshots are not committed.

## 1. What the unit is

The unit is the heads-up display and a group of small screens that the day, weekday, holiday and telephone modes share. It contains no mode handler of its own. A mode handler calls an `*_init` function once to create actors (sprites) and a `*_show` or `*_update` function every frame. Everything is drawn through the actor pool and the sprite table of the graphics layer ([[spec/h-graphics]]); coordinates are screen coordinates with the origin in the middle of the 320 x 240 screen (x -160..159, y -120..119).

## 2. The day screen as built by this unit

Layout seen on the weekday run screen (screenshot of the emulator state, all positions in screen coordinates):

- Top: the parameter panel, three rows of three parameters (section 5), rectangle 264 x 42 at (-132, -97).
- Right edge: the date panel, rectangle 20 x 114 at (134, -75) with year, month, day and weekday (section 6).
- Bottom: the message window, rectangle 266 x 52 at (-133, 46) (section 7).
- Left: the command icons in a block from x -138 to -66, y -41 to 31 (section 8).
- Behind everything: a scrolling check pattern drawn by `screen_frame_draw` (section 11); in the middle the scene background (`dec_bg_draw`, other unit).
- The pointer (actor 0, core `cursor_*`) is drawn by the cursor functions.

Per-frame call lists:

- day screen (mode 0x31, `mode_day_screen`): the queue reader or ending start, `parameter_show`, `message_window_show`, `hizuke_show`, `icon_bar_update(0)`, `mascot_update`, `dec_bg_draw`, `screen_frame_draw(1)`.
- weekday run screen (mode 0x40, `week_day`): the stage handler, `parameter_show`, `message_window_show`, `hizuke_show`, `icon_bar_update(1)`, `dec_bg_draw(0)`, `screen_frame_draw(1)` and, when actor 27 is visible, `mascot_update`.
- command screen (mode 0x34, `mode_command_screen`): the stage handler (selection stage: `parameter_show`, `hizuke_show`, `message_window_show`, `icon_bar_update(0)`, cursor update, `message_window_title_update`, `dec_bg_draw`), `mascot_update` when visible, `screen_frame_draw(0)`; stage 2 is the calendar check and calls `calendar_view_update`.

Emulator trace from the weekday run state, 145 frames: `parameter_show`, `hizuke_show`, `message_window_show`, `icon_bar_update`, `hud_season_index` and `screen_frame_draw` ran every frame; `parameter_show_init`, `hizuke_init`, `message_window_init`, `hud_icon_bar_init`, `hud_icon_layout_apply` and `hud_mascot_set` ran once or twice at entry; `mascot_update` ran once; `portrait_face_update` did not run.

Typical init sequence (boot HUD init, holiday handler, weekday init, command screen): `hizuke_init`, `parameter_show_init` twice, `hud_icon_bar_init`, `message_window_init`. The double call has this effect: the second call finds the snapshot equal to the live values and so draws every digit plainly (no highlight, section 5). A single call right after a parameter change (after `parameter_up_down`) shows the changes.

## 4. Portrait face (bustup)

The portrait of the girl in a scene is built by core's `portrait_build`: five image blocks in texture slots 5-9, a frame and sequence table, two animation drivers (actors 30 and 31) and six sprite quads. This unit animates the face.

- `portrait_read_start(lba)` (core) reads 0x2D sectors to 0x80180000 and clears `D_8011F50F`, `D_8011F4CB` (the drivers' draw flags) and `mouth_frames_reversed`.
- `portrait_expression_select(rank)` (called from `portrait_build`; the callers pass the girl's mood rank `get_g_zyotai_h(current_girl) & 0x7F`, 0 best to 4 worst; the loaders at lines 2264 and 2275 of `8007C030.c` and DATE force it to 0 for girl ids 12 and up, KANGEI and the loader at line 2291 do not, and ETC passes its own value 0-5): the eye and mouth quads (0, 1) take their picture from texture page 7 for rank 0-1, 8 for 2-3, 9 for 4; an odd rank shifts the V origin by 0x40 (the second half of the block). So a girl shows one of five expression sets that follows her relationship rank.
- `portrait_face_update()`, every frame while a portrait is shown: sets driver 31 to brightness 0x80 with its hidden bit cleared and unhides quads 0 and 1, then
  - voice on (`voice_mode != 0`): the CD audio level (`cd_state_get() & 4` means audio is playing; level = `getCDlevel() + 1`, else 0) is stored in the 4-entry ring `cd_level_history[frame_count & 3]`; the mouth frame n (0-3) is the number of thresholds of `portrait_lipsync_thresholds[current_girl]` that are below the level (the largest satisfied threshold wins, max 3); quad 0 V = 16 * n. The mouth frame is recomputed every frame when it is 0 and only on even frames when open. Holding square clears `lipsync_peak_level`; the peak is not read.
  - voice off: quad 0 V = 16 * frame index of driver 31 (the scripted talk animation).
  - quad 1 (eyes) V = 32 * frame index of driver 30 (blink animation).
- `bustup_speech()`: if voice is off and driver 31's animation bit 0 is clear, sets bits 0 and 4 (start the talk loop). `bustup_wink()`: the same for driver 30 (blink loop).
- Lip-sync thresholds (u8 [16][3] per `GIRL_*` id, levels above which the mouth shows frame 1, 2, 3): shiori 16/40/56, mio 24/52/72, yuina 16/40/56, ayako 24/56/80, saki 16/40/64, yukari 14/36/56, nozomi 12/28/36, mira 20/40/64, yuko 16/36/56, megumi 16/32/44, yumi 14/36/64, miharu 16/32/48, rei 16/32/44, yoshio 12/28/40, sotoi 16/32/48, id 15 16/32/48.
- Event picture counterparts. A full-screen event still in a dialogue scene (`D_80122D04` nonzero, called `scene_event_still_visible` in the dates-b table) has a mouth layer (actor 96) and an eyes layer (actor 97), animated by three functions of this unit that mirror the portrait. Core's per-frame `bustup_wink_update` picks the branch: portrait shown (`D_80122CF4`, `scene_girl_visible` there) runs `portrait_face_update` and winks every 240 frames; event still shown runs the event branch below; with neither shown no animation function runs. In the event branch: with the mouth mode `D_80122D10` (`scene_event_mouth_anim`) equal to 0 it calls `portrait_event_face_mouth_lipsync()` every frame; with 1 it calls `portrait_event_face_mouth_animation_start()` every 240 frames; and every 240 frames, unless the eyes hold flag `D_80122D0C` (`scene_event_eyes_hold`) is set, it calls `portrait_event_face_eyes_animation_start()`.
  - `portrait_event_face_mouth_lipsync()` is the lip sync of the portrait applied to actor 96's frame: only while `voice_mode != 0`, frame n (0-3) = number of thresholds of `portrait_lipsync_thresholds[current_girl]` below the CD audio level (ring `cd_level_history`), recomputed on every frame while the frame is 0 and only on even frames while it is open.
  - `portrait_event_face_mouth_animation_start()` and `portrait_event_face_eyes_animation_start()` start the animation of actor 96 (mouth) or 97 (eyes) by setting bits 0 and 1 of `anim_flags` when bit 0 is clear. Other callers: `dialogue_run` (core) calls the mouth start for lines typed without voice while the event still is shown and the mouth mode is 0 (the portrait uses `bustup_speech` there); GEKO calls the eyes start at frame count % 240 == 120 and SHUGAKU after it clears the hold flag.
  - Evidence for the event reading (and against the earlier SD sprite guess): the only callers are the event branch above, GEKO and SHUGAKU; the branch mirrors the portrait's mouth and blink; the O.BIN names `event_face_kuchi0/1` (mouth only, writes actor 96) and `ev_me_on` (eyes, writes actor 97) in the dates-b table pair the actors with mouth and eyes. Core's guess that actors 96 and 97 also carry SD characters on the weekday and holiday screens (the `sd_anim_fast` note) is a separate question: none of these three functions runs there, and it stays a HYPOTHESIS in core.
- `mouth_frames_reversed` (`D_800B5A60`, HYPOTHESIS name; the dates-b table adopted this name and calls the swap partner `D_800CA360` `mouth_frames_reversed_saved`): its only reader is ENDING's lip sync, which sets it itself (1 for girl 0 in scene 8, else 0) and then uses `3 - level` while it is set. Writers: DATE (1 before `event_face0`, 0 after `event_face1`), GEKO and SHOUGATU (1 with their event pictures), `portrait_read_start` (0); the event still show/hide function (`func_80085234`) swaps it with `D_800CA360`. Nothing else reads it.
- Dead code: `portrait_hide(arg)` (hides drivers and quads 0-5 when `arg == 0`, never called). `portrait_value_clear_if_high` (called by `portrait_build`, `portrait_layout_set`, GEKO and SHUGAKU) compares `D_800B5A64` with 0xA5 and zeroes it, but nothing increments that byte (initial value 0), so the call has no effect.

## 5. Parameter panel

State: the nine `param_*` records of `GameState` (16.16 values, value 0..999, core 4.1) and the 0x7C-byte `profile_snapshot` inside `progress_state` (new struct `ProgressState`: `place_flags[21]` at +0x00, `command_counts[9]` at +0x15, two unused bytes, `profile_snapshot[0x7C]` at +0x20; header change for the apply step). The snapshot holds a copy of `GameState` +0xC4..+0x13F (names, flags, birthday and the nine parameters).

- `parameter_change(i, delta)`: `param[i].word += delta` (16.16), a negative word becomes 0, a value of 1000 or more becomes 999. `parameter_up_down` (weekday unit) calls it.
- `parameter_show_init()`: loads five palette rows to VRAM (0x120, 0x1E0..0x1E4), creates actors 32..59, then for each parameter k (0..8) writes three digit actors: frame `0x44 + digit` (digit from `decimal_digit(value, 3/2/1)`: hundreds, tens, units), spacing 8 px. Grid: column k % 3 at x = -73, 15, 103 (x of the first digit), row k / 3 at y = -88, -74, -60. Actor 59 (frame 0x3D) is the label at (0, -72).
  - If the live value is larger than the snapshot value, the three digits get `clut_row` `HUD_CLUT_UP_SATURDAY` (0x43) and flip phase 1; if smaller, `HUD_CLUT_DOWN_SUNDAY` (0x44) and phase 1; equal values stay plain.
  - At the end the live profile block is copied into `profile_snapshot` (so the next call compares against the values shown now).
- `parameter_show()`, every frame: while actor 32 is visible it draws the panel background (`prim_box_gouraud(-132, -97, 264, 42, slot 12, colour 0x30E00)`) and runs the flip animation of the digit actors: phase 1 shrinks `scale_x` by `0x1000 / step` per frame (and raises `anim_timer`) until it reaches that step, then phase 2 grows it back and the phase returns to 0. `step` is 8, or 4 when `sd_anim_fast` is nonzero. The stress digits (actors 56-58) use `scale_y` instead.
- `parameter_disp_switch(1/0)` shows or hides actors 32-59.

The digits show the new value at once; the flip and the colour only mark the change.

## 6. Date panel

`hizuke_init()` creates actors 16..23 and sets: year digits (frame `0x44 + digit`, year % 100) at x = 140 and 148, y = -65; month at y = -34; day at y = -1; weekday sprite frame `0x52 + weekday` at (146, 31); label actor 16 (frame 0x3C) at (144, -18). A month or day below 10 hides the tens digit actor (`draw_flags = 0`, parked at (255, 255)) and puts the units digit at x = 144. The weekday sprite colour: `clut_row` 0x43 on Saturday, 0x44 on Sunday, and 0x44 on any day whose `day_kind` (this month's record, day index = today) is 1 or 2 (Sunday or national holiday). `hizuke_show()` draws the background rectangle (134, -75, 20 x 114, slot 5, colour 0x30E00) while actor 16 is visible. `hizuke_disp_switch(1/0)` shows or hides the eight actors.

## 7. Message window and title banner

Message window: `message_window_init()` creates actor 1 (frame 0x3E at (0, 75), priority 7), `message_window_show()` draws the rectangle (-133, 46, 266 x 52, slot 8, colour 0x30E00) while it is visible, `message_disp_switch` toggles it. The text itself is the text list (`set_kanji_string`, other unit).

Title banner (top left, a short screen name): `message_window_set_title(text)` (core) queues the text at (-206, -152), sets `title_banner_length = strlen / 2` and builds `length + 2` plate actors from slot 3: the right cap (frame 0x73) in slot 3, `length` tiles (frame 0x72), the left cap (frame 0x71) in the last slot; piece i sits at x = (length - i) * 14 - 135 plus the slide offset and y = -81 plus the slide offset. `message_window_title_update()` runs the timeline kept in `title_banner_timer`:

| timer | what |
|---|---|
| -0x40 .. -1 | slides in from the top left; the text entry follows; the timer rises by 1 per frame |
| 0 .. 0x77 | shown at home (plate at (-144, -90), 20 px high, width `length * 14 + 8`) |
| 0x78 .. 0xB7 | slides out |
| 0xB8 | retired (off screen); while triangle is held the banner is shown at home |

`message_window_set_title_hidden(text)` is `set_title` followed by `title_banner_timer = 0xB8` (the banner starts retired; the original callers pass the string through the register although the C prototype is `(void)`). `message_window_title_show(1/0)` shows or hides the plate actors and the text entry (it also resets the timer to retired when hiding).

## 8. Command icon bar

Fourteen icons (`ICON_COUNT_FIXED`) in the two fixed layouts, fifteen (`ICON_COUNT_FREE`) in the free layout; `icon_mode` (`ICON_MODE_TYPE_A/B/FREE`) and `icon_count` are saved settings. Each icon is two actors: a base plate (slot 64 + i, frame 2) and a glyph (slot 80 + i, sequence i). Icons 0-7 are the weekday commands (`COMMAND_LIBERAL_ARTS` .. `COMMAND_REST`, the scheduler unit's `WeekdayCommand`); the other icons are handled by the command screen (mode 0x34) and the holiday handler (mode 0x46), which own their names. Command screen: icons 8-10 also store their number as the chosen command and go to the schedule step; icon 12 opens the calendar check (stage 2); icon 13 opens the data (memory card) screen (stage 3; its glyph uses sequence 14 while `card_busy` is set and sequence 13 otherwise, `hud_icon_memory_card_animation_set`); icon 11 opens the system settings (stage 4); icon 14 exists only in the free layout, stores the value 0xE ("no command") and goes to the schedule step, with a notice when an exam or the school trip starts the next day. Holiday handler: 0-7 commands, 8 stage 0x10, 10 stage 0x20, 12 stage 0x30, 11 stage 0x40, 9 the appointment of the day (only when one is due today).

State: `icon_enabled[15]` (`icon_can_use_set(i, on)` stores 1 or 0; `get_icon_can_use(i)` returns 1 only when it is 1), `icon_positions[15][2]` (s16 x, y), `icon_selected_prev`, `icon_pulse_timer`.

- `hud_icon_bar_init()`: in free layout `icon_count` becomes 15; `icon_enabled[0 .. icon_count - 1] = 1`; creates the plate actors (frame 2, priority 4) and glyph actors (sequence = icon index, priority 3); copies positions: type A from `icon_layout_a`, type B from `icon_layout_b`, free layout keeps the saved array, but if two or more of its first 15 positions are (0, 0) (never saved) it restores layout A; calls `hud_icon_layout_apply(icon_count)`; loads three palette rows (VRAM 0x1F1, 0x1F2, 0x1F3).
- `hud_icon_layout_apply(n)`: `n` other than 15 becomes 14 (stored back to `icon_count`); registers menu 0 with `n` hit boxes of 20 x 16 at (x - 10, y - 10); moves plate and glyph actors to the positions; actors 78 and 94 (icon 14) are hidden unless `n == 15`.
- `icon_bar_update(a)`, every frame:
  1. If L2 is held and `icon_mode == 2`: the d-pad moves the selected icon (`menu_cursor_0`) and the pointer by `cursor_speed` pixels per frame, only if `hud_icon_move_allowed` accepts the move; `hud_icon_layout_apply` refreshes the boxes and then the stored icon position is clamped to x -138..128, y -92..92 (the actors follow on the next refresh). With L2 held in layouts A and B nothing happens. (In the menu modes that step by pad, `menu_mode` 1 and 3, the d-pad moves `menu_cursor_0` over the enabled icons one per press, wrapping, and the pointer jumps next to the icon: emulator, from icon 0 two presses of left reach icon 13 and then icon 12.) Without L2: `menu_check(0, pointer x, pointer y)` and `menu_cursor_update(0)` (hover detection).
  2. `hud_icon_move_allowed(i, dx, dy)` returns 0 when the moved icon's box leaves x -160..160 or its top edge leaves y -120..120 (the bottom edge is not tested: the code repeats the top test), or when a corner of the 20 x 16 box falls inside another icon's box.
  3. Selection change: when `menu_cursor_0 != icon_selected_prev`, `icon_pulse_timer = 0x20` (it counts down).
  4. Per icon: disabled -> glyph `anim_flags = 0`, plate `clut_row = HUD_CLUT_ICON_DISABLED` (0x51), plate priority 3. Enabled -> plate priority 4, glyph priority 3; hovered (`menu_items[i][0].hover != 0`) -> plate `clut_row` 0x52, glyph priority 2, plate priority 3, and when `a == 0` the glyph scale is `0x1000 + rsin((0x20 - timer) << 6)` on both axes (a pulse after a selection change); otherwise plate `clut_row` 0x53 and scale 0x1000.
  5. Icon 13's animation: sequence 14 (frame reset when >= 4) while `card_busy`, else sequence 13 (frame reset when >= 6).
  `a != 0` (17 call sites) is the display-only form of the bar: no pulse.
- Layouts (x, y in screen coordinates; `icon_layout_a`: 15 entries): A = (-138,-41) (-114,-41) (-90,-41) (-138,-25) (-90,-25) (-138,-9) (-114,-9) (-90,-9) (-138,15) (-114,15) (-90,15) (-138,31) (-114,31) (-90,31) (-66,31). B = (-138,-45) (-114,-45) (-138,-29) (-114,-29) (-138,-13) (-114,-13) (-138,3) (-114,3) (-138,19) (-114,19) (-90,19) (-138,35) (-114,35) (-90,35) (-66,35). Emulator: the weekday run state held layout A in `icon_positions` and only icon 0 enabled; the command screen state of 1995-04-09 (first selection) had icons 0-3, 5-7 and 11-13 enabled (club icon 4 off for a player without a club, 8-10 and 14 off).
- `icon_disp_switch(1/0)` shows or hides the plate and glyph actors of the first `icon_count` icons.

## 11. Background check pattern (`screen_frame_draw`)

Core named it. Each frame it advances a scroll offset (`checker_scroll_u/v`) by a rule that depends on the player's health state (`player_flags` bits 8-11): 0 (and anything else) full speed (v - 1 every 2nd frame, u + 1 every frame); 1 about half; 2 about a quarter; 3 about an eighth; 4 random jitter of +-16 per frame; 5 a sine and cosine swirl. Then it draws a 10 x 8 grid of 32 x 32 px sprites (texture page 0x3C0, 0x100, uv offset by the scroll, grey tint = `screen_brightness`, CLUT at VRAM (0xA0, 0x1E1), ordering slot `checker_ot_slot`), skipping cells by the mask argument: 0 draws all 80, 1 leaves out columns 1-8 of rows 1-5 (the screen border only), 2 leaves out rows 0-5 except the two edge cells of row 5 (the lower two rows and the edges above them). Call counts: argument 2 at 38 sites, 1 at 22, 0 at 12.

Seasonal colour: `season_palettes[4]` (one 16-colour row per `hud_season_index`) are blended into the CLUT by a 16-step crossfade (weights `255 - fade` and `fade - 239`, shifted right by 4, one step every 4 frames) that runs only while `season_palette_fade` (`D_800B5BC8`) is 0xEF or more; a normal frame leaves it at 0. The step is taken once per four frames (frame count & 3 == 3) and adds 1. Steps 240 to 254 blend the palette of the previous season (index (season + 3) & 3) into the current one, the new palette's weight being `fade - 239` of 16; step 255 loads the current palette whole and the byte then wraps to 0. Who writes it:

- 0xEF starts the full 16-step crossfade (about one second). The month-start step of the scheduler unit (`month_data_load_step`, `src/main/8006CB30.c`) writes it when the new month is 3, 6, 9 or 12, i.e. the first month of a season, in every case of that branch (the branch reloads the day music with `hud_day_bgm_load`, except on 1996-09-01; seasons: spring 3-5, summer 6-8, autumn 9-11, winter 12-2); the OPTION overlay writes it after it reloads the month images with `advance` set.
- 0xFE makes the next step jump straight to the final palette (one step, no visible fade): the command screen setup (`src/main/8005A0B0.c`, after `calendar_month_load`), BUNKASAI and BUNKAKEN when they leave (after the month images are reloaded), and NAME_ENT on entry (stage 0).

So the season palette changes at the first day of month 3, 6, 9 and 12 with a short crossfade, and every screen that rebuilds the background snaps to the right palette.

## 12. Biorhythm and the command roll

`hud_biorhythm_value(physical, emotional, intellectual, scale)` evaluates three sine waves: phase `p = hud_biorhythm_birth_offset() + day_count` (day_count is the u16 `GameState` day counter; callers may add days ahead by temporarily adding to it), waves `rsin(p * 0xB2)`, `rsin(p * 0x92)`, `rsin(p * 0x7C)`; one rsin turn is 4096, so the periods are 4096 / 178 = 23 days, 28 days and 33 days (the classic biorhythm cycles; the labels follow the commands each wave judges, section below). The three flags select which waves count.

- For each selected wave w: count + 1; if |w| < 0x199 (a critical day) total -= 0x1000 and bad + 1; else if w > 0 total += w; else total -= w / 2 (a negative wave adds half its magnitude, as coded).
- `scale > 0`: if total / count < 0 return 0, else `(total * scale / 4096) / count`. `scale < 0`: the first selected wave times scale / 4096 (the graph uses -32). `scale == 0` (no caller): -1 when a selected wave is critical (with all three selected: when at least two are), else 1.
- Callers: the command roll (`hud_command_roll_poor_result`, below), the unused probe, and ETC's result computation (`src/ovl/ETC/80146400.c`), which calls it with scale 16 for different wave selections and prints the terms `main`, `sub`, `bio` and `ret` in its debug text; that debug label is the best in-code support for the name.
- `hud_biorhythm_birth_offset()`: `5475 - birthday_day_table[birth month] - birth day`; for a birthday on April 1-3: `5506 - birthday_day_table[3] - day`. `birthday_day_table` (u16 [13]): 0, 95, 64, 35, 370, 340, 309, 279, 248, 217, 187, 156, 126 (days from the first of the month to a reference date near April 5 of the next occurrence). It is a phase constant only; the sign of the day term is as coded.

`hud_command_roll_poor_result(days_ahead)` decides whether a weekday command goes poorly. It returns 1 (poor) or 0 (normal); the queue builder stores it in the school-day record's scene field and the holiday handler in the record of the chosen command, and `parameter_up_down` halves the positive parameter gains of a poor result and adds one extra point of stress (its stress term is the scaled row value plus `arg2 - 1`, with `arg2` 1 for a normal and 2 for a poor result). Steps (as coded, `day_count` is temporarily advanced by `days_ahead` and restored):

1. base by `last_command` low nibble: 0 liberal arts: `hud_biorhythm_value(0,0,1,16) + liberal arts / 16`; 1 science: same wave + science / 16; 2 art: `(0,1,0,16) + art / 16`; 3 athletics: `(1,0,0,16) + athletics / 16`; 4 club: wave by the player's club (clubs 0 literature, 2 science, 3 computer: intellectual; 1 drama, 4 art, 5 brass band: emotional; 6-10 sports: physical) `+ club exp / 16` (the club record's 16-bit exp shifted right 4); 5 trivia: `(0,1,0,16) + trivia / 16`; 6 looks: `(0,1,0,16) + looks / 16`; 7 rest: a probe of all three waves is evaluated and dropped, base = 16.
2. `score = base + 6`; `stress factor f` = 4 when stress >= 97, else stress / 32; `score = rand() % (score + 4)`; `score = (8 - f) * score / 8`.
3. Three probes at scale 3: physical, emotional and intellectual wave alone; each probe that returns 0 counts as a failure (a critical wave, or a contribution that rounds to 0). If all three fail, `score /= 4`.
4. Return 1 when `score < 4`, else 0.

Emulator: `break` on `hud_command_roll_poor_result` during the first weekday run showed it called with 1 from the holiday handler (`func_80076000` below `mode_holiday`) and with 0 from `day_events_build_week` (via `schedule_init`); inside it `hud_biorhythm_value(0,0,1,16)` then the three scale-3 probes ran.

The wave labels: command 3 and the sports clubs use the 23-day wave, arts and trivia/looks the 28-day wave, the study commands the 33-day wave (HYPOTHESIS for the words physical, emotional, intellectual; the mapping is code).

`hud_biorhythm_graph_draw()` (calendar check page): a pulsing grey zero line from (-128, -12) to (124, -12), then three polylines of 16 points each, one per wave, for the days `day_count - 8 .. day_count + 7` (the graph calls `hud_biorhythm_value(.., -32)`, so a wave is plotted as `-sin * 32 / 4096` pixels around the zero line, 16 px per day, x from -144); the line colours cycle with `(i + frame_count) & 15`. `calendar_view_update()` calls it first. Emulator screenshot of the calendar check page: the three curves (green, red, blue in the picture) cross both month pages from the left edge of the notebook to about x = 100.

Used by the command screen (mode 0x34) calendar check (icon 12, stage 2) and by TEL's "choose the day for a date" step. Per frame: `calendar_view_update()` = `hud_biorhythm_graph_draw`, `dtd_on_tpage(0, 0, 1, 2, 0)`, `cal_base_show()` (panel (-160, -87), 283 x 125, slot 5, colour 0xE0E0E0) and the mark pulse: `t = marks[0].anim_timer++`; for actors 0x61..0x9F, phase `(t >> 6) & 3`: 0 scale = (t & 63) << 6 (grow), 1 scale 0x1000, 2 scale = (63 - (t & 63)) << 6 (shrink), 3 scale 0 (hidden); the cycle is 256 frames.

Month data. `month_calendar[2]` (core) holds this month and next month: `day_kind[0]` = days in the month, `day_kind[d]` = `DayKind`, `marker[0]` = weekday of the 1st (0 Monday .. 6 Sunday), `marker[d]` = `CalendarMarker`. `calendar_month_load(year, month, advance)`: `idx = (year - 95) * 12 + month - 4`; when `idx < 35` it uploads the two 32 x 128 images of blobs `idx` and `idx + 1` of `month_data_table` to VRAM (0x3A0, 0x100) and (0x3A0, 0x180); when `advance != 0` it also copies record 1 to record 0 (0x40 bytes) and loads record 1 from blob `idx + 1` (day count, first weekday, then that many kinds and markers). `advance == 1` is used at month changes and in the OPTION date setup; `advance == 0` reloads the images after an overlay overwrote VRAM (BUNKAKEN, BUNKASAI, TAIIKU, TACO, the command screen).

Cells. `calendar_cell_x(side, d)` = `side * 0x88 + column * 16 - 0x78`, `calendar_cell_y(side, d)` = `row * 16 - 0x30`, where `side` 0 is this month and 1 next month, `c = (first + d + 1)` with `first` = `marker[0]` of the side (a Sunday first day, 6, counts as -1) and `d` the 0-based day; column = `c % 7`, row = `c / 7`. Columns run Sunday to Saturday. TEL uses them minus 16 as 16 x 16 pick boxes for the days of the next 28 days that are Sundays or holidays (`day_kind` 1 or 2) without an appointment.

Marks (`calendar_marks_build`): clears 0x20 actors from slot 0x61 (draw_flags 0x8C, direct frame mode, brightness 0x80, off screen), then in this order, adding one actor per mark (stop when slot 0xA0 is reached):

1. player birthday: `CAL_FRAME_PLAYER_BIRTHDAY` (0x6E) on this month's cell when the birthday month is this month, on next month's cell when it is month + 1 (no December wrap here).
2. girls 0-10 with `birthday_known` (flag bit 6): `CAL_FRAME_GIRL_BIRTHDAY` (0x6D) in the same way, with the December to January wrap.
3. the eight appointment slots, if active: `CAL_FRAME_DATE` (0x62) for this month and for `month % 12 + 1`.
4. sports-club members (club id 6-10, bits 12-15 of `player_flags`): `CAL_FRAME_CLUB_EVENT` (0x6F) on the third Sunday, day `21 - weekday of the 1st` (this month; next month's too, except in February 1998).
5. per day of this month (then next month): when `marker` is not 0 and `calendar_marker_frames[marker]` is not 0xFF the frame is marked, except frame 0x6F which is only marked in August (this month 8 / next month when this month is 7) and only for players who have a club. A frame of the kinds `CAL_FRAME_EXAM` (not when the month is February), `CAL_FRAME_SCHOOL_TRIP` or the August 0x6F repeats on the following days that have no marker of their own for 6 days in total, `CAL_FRAME_WEEK_EVENT` (0x67) for 7 days.
6. past encounters: every `event_log` entry of the current year and month marks its day with 0x62; girl 12 entries only when her `SystemData.flags[12]` or `girl_cleared[12]` is set.

`calendar_marker_frames[marker]` (24 entries, 0xFF = none): 2, 3, 10, 16, 21 -> 0x65 (notice frame); 4 sports festival -> 0x64; 5 exam -> 0x66; 8 club season -> 0x6F; 11 school trip -> 0x6C; 12 culture festival -> 0x63; 13 Christmas preparation -> 0x6B; 15 New Year preparation -> 0x6A; 17 Valentine -> 0x68; 18 career choice -> 0x66; 19 seasonal party -> 0x69; 23 -> 0x67; the others 0xFF (core 3.2 names the markers).

## 14. Information magazine and date places

A quarterly page (the holiday handler titles it the information magazine) that unlocks one new date place per issue. Eight of the thirteen issues carry a place.

- Issue number from the date (`hud_magazine_page_build`, not always a bit written by `hud_magazine_mark_read`; see its year gates below): months 2-4 -> `4 * (year - 95)`, months 5-7 -> `4 * (year - 95) + 1`, months 8-10 -> `+ 2`, months 11-12 -> `+ 3`, month 1 -> `4 * (year - 96) + 3`; so issue k covers the three months starting at month `(3k + 2) % 12 + 1` of the page title (issue 0: March to May, issue 1: June to August, issue 3: December to February); issue 12 is the spring of 1998.
- `hud_magazine_mark_read()` (the magazine step calls it first): sets issue bits 0..11 only for years 95..97 in month groups 2-4, 5-7, 8-10, and 11-13; January 96..98 sets preceding-year bits 3/7/11. Spring 1998 has no issue-bit-12 write. It also sets bit 14 except in months 2, 5, 8, 11 where it sets bit 13, bit 15 in months 5-7 (TEL offers place 18, `PLACE_SHRINE`, in the first week of August only when bit 15 is set), and both bits 13 and 14 in April 1995. `quarter_flags_rotate` (core) moves bit 13 to bit 14 at months 3, 6, 9, 12.
- `hud_magazine_page_build()`: lays out the page for the issue: a header actor, up to ten layers chosen by quarter and year (first layer 0x79..0x7D, then variant frames by year), the page title and a second heading (`magazine_title`, `magazine_subtitle`, 13 strings each), three text lines (`magazine_lines`), three month labels `number_strings[(3k + 2) % 12 + 1 ...]` at x = 256 (a second page off the right edge of the screen, HYPOTHESIS: the magazine scrolls), three symbols `0x90 + magazine_spot_marks[k][i]`, and the narration with the player name; issues 0, 9-10 and 11-12 instead show one of three two-line notices.
- Place unlock inside `hud_magazine_page_build` (sets `place_flags[p] = 1`): issue 1 -> place 1 (`PLACE_KIRAMEKI_CENTRAL_PARK`) unless month 5; issue 2 -> 7 (`PLACE_ART_MUSEUM`) unless month 8; issue 3 -> 3 (`PLACE_AQUARIUM`) in December and unconditionally in January; issue 4 -> 13 (`PLACE_STADIUM`) unless month 2; issue 5 -> 5 (`PLACE_BOTANICAL_GARDEN`) unless month 5; issue 6 -> 10 (`PLACE_BOWLING_ALLEY`) unless month 8; issue 7 -> 6 (`PLACE_PLANETARIUM`) in December and in January; issue 8 -> 11 (`PLACE_KARAOKE`) unless month 2; issues 0, 9, 10, 11, 12 none.
- Scheduler side (the scheduler unit's `func_8006D138`, noted here for the port): in the second and third month of a quarter a set issue bit stores 1 in `place_flags`; in the first month after the quarter, an unset bit stores `PLACE_FLAG_PENDING` (0x80); TEL's phone scene then announces one pending place, sets the issue bit (bits 1-8 for places 1, 7, 3, 13, 5, 10, 6, 11) and stores 1. Date panels elsewhere read `place_flags[p] & 0x7F` as "place p can be chosen" (TEL).
