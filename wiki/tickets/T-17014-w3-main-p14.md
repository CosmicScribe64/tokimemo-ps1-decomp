### 2026-10-17 claude-opus (closer pass)

Closer pass on the one guarded near miss, about 35 minutes: `strNext` (496 bytes) is matched and committed (`T-17014: match main:strNext`). The function has no guard now; the build is 27/27 SHA1 OK.

- regdiff on the draft without `w` showed why the local is needed. The stream width is a cross-block CSE expression (`ilod(D_80125C58)`, priority 1.667). With no local it takes `$v0`. With `w` it takes `$a0`, and `w` (priority 2) holds `$v0` although no instruction reads or writes it: uopt copy-propagates the CSE temporary into the compare but keeps the range. So the original has a local like this, and the remaining problem was only the operand order.
- Writing the expressions without a local the other way round (`D_80125C58->width != sector->width`) already gives the original's order: for these two expressions the C operands come out reversed (the height compare does the same; the header compare keeps the C order). A plain variable always goes first. A no-op arithmetic wrapper turns `w` back into an expression: `w * 1 != sector->width` matches, and so do `w | 0`, `w ^ 0`, `w << 0` and `w & -1` (all on the left; on the right they keep `bne a0,t0`). `w + 0`, `w - 0` and `+w` keep the plain-variable order. The `* 1` form is committed with a FAKE comment.
- Dead ends: `w` read only inside the else arm (`w = D_80125C58->width; env->rect[0].w = env->rect[1].w = w;`) also keeps `$v0` free of instructions and fixes the width register, but moves the ugen temporaries of the tail by one (7 rows; autotweak, 237 edits: no change). `w` set at the join, or in the 24-bit arm, gives a real `move` (125 instructions). `sector->width - w != 0`, `(sector->width ^ w) != 0` and `sector->width - w` give the right order but cost two ugen temporaries (28 rows). Moving the declaration of `w` above `rect` shifts the stack slots, and a second scalar after `rect` makes the frame 0x60.
- `strCallback` and `func_80056BA8` were not in this pass (still `INCLUDE_ASM`, drafts in `drafts/T-17014/`).
